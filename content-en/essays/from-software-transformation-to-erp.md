# Abstract
This article presents a step-by-step model for how an organisation's software needs evolve until it reaches a successful Enterprise Resource Planning (ERP) deployment. Drawing on field experience in real projects and an analysis of process structures, it tries to show that ERP creates value only when the organisation has reached a certain level of operational complexity, data maturity and cultural readiness. The proposed model has five stages, and for each stage it defines transition indicators, suitable tools and likely obstacles. Two case studies, one successful and one partly successful application of the model in Iranian organisations, show its practical use.

# Introduction
In recent decades the pace of change in information technology has pushed organisations to rethink traditional management. Competitiveness, productivity and quick response to the market no longer depend only on people and physical equipment; they require the intelligent use of data, information systems and technology-based decision-making.

## Why organisations need digital transformation
Digital transformation starts at the level of tools and automation but, at a deeper level, means redesigning processes, organisational structure and decision culture. To survive in complex, dynamic markets, organisations have to turn their data into operational and strategic capital, which is possible only through integrated, intelligent systems.

## Where enterprise software sits in structural maturity
Enterprise software is the bridge between operational processes and strategic goals. In the early stages of maturity, organisations use simple tools such as Excel or accounting software. As complexity grows and cross-unit coordination is needed, MIS, integrated financial systems and finally ERP come into play. ERP is not only a tool but a platform for changing the mental and structural model of the organisation.

## Aim of the article: a model of software transformation up to ERP
The main aim is to present a developmental model for recognising the right time to enter ERP and for designing the path to it. By focusing on five key stages of software transformation, the model helps managers invest in ERP intelligently and at the right time, and avoid costly failures.

# Literature review

## Types of enterprise information systems
Depending on maturity, purpose and breadth of function, information systems in organisations fall into these categories:
- MIS: supports day-to-day operations, record keeping, correspondence, reporting and internal communication
- ERP: integrates the organisation's processes and data at the macro level, with financial, human resources, production, sales and other modules
- BPMS: designs, runs, monitors and optimises cross-unit processes, either standalone or alongside ERP

## Organisational maturity models

- **CMMI** (Capability Maturity Model Integration): a reference model for measuring an organisation's process maturity on five levels, from initial to continuously optimising
- **Nolan model**: describes the stages of information-system maturity in organisations, from initial awareness to full integration

## Challenges and benefits of ERP in growing organisations

Benefits:
- Cross-unit coordination and fewer process bottlenecks
- Real-time access to data and management analysis
- Less rework and fewer human errors

Challenges:
- Cultural unreadiness and staff resistance
- Hidden costs of implementation and maintenance
- Complexity in selecting, adapting and localising processes

# Conceptual framework

## The five stages of software transformation
1. Communication and simple automation (MIS)
2. Financial and functional recording (accounting tools)
3. Connected task modules
4. Process-oriented ERP
5. Future-planning ERP (strategic and integrated)

## Indicators of transition between stages

- Growth in data volume and variety of tasks
- A sudden rise in staff or projects
- Failure of traditional tools (such as Excel)
- A growing need to coordinate independent units

## Barriers to transition and conditions for success

- Barriers: traditional culture, neglect of data, excessive tool-centrism
- Conditions for success: training, change management, process modelling before ERP, choosing a tool that fits the organisation's maturity

**_The proposed model:_** The model proposed here is a five-stage roadmap for organisations' software transformation that pays attention not only to tools but also to cultural structure, process and decision-making. It was built not only on theoretical concepts but on field studies, and it helps managers recognise the right time for ERP and avoid extra costs and common failures.

# Methodology
This article was developed with a case-study-based model-building approach. The data come from the author's field experience in direct work with several Iranian organisations in different industries, including publishing, telecommunications equipment and technical services. For confidentiality the organisations are not named, but their structure, processes and decisions are described in a detailed analysis.
The data were analysed by matching real situations against the five-stage framework of software transformation. The transition indicators, barriers and remedies were also designed through empirical observation and comparison. The model's validity was assessed through its fit with practical results and feedback from managers in these organisations.

## Case studies
**_Case 1: a publishing organisation and the analysis of financial processes._** This organisation had high physical capacity (an advanced print shop) but low income. A first analysis showed that data flow between sales, finance and warehouse was inefficient and unsystematic. After analysing customer demand, working out the unit cost and designing KPIs, the need for ERP disappeared, and with a simple structure of Excel and Power BI, data transparency and decision-making improved.
**_Case 2: an electronic equipment organisation and gradual ERP implementation._** After staff grew from 100 to 250, this organisation met a severe break in its processes. First a culture of documentation was strengthened and formal and informal communications were identified. Then, by defining product management processes and designing control indicators, the ERP implementation path began. After benchmarking and comparing ERPs, Odoo was chosen as a modular system, and gradual phasing was carried out in the key units.

## Lessons learned and fit with the model
- Cultural maturity and documentation come before ERP.
- ERP makes sense when processes are modelled and data is structured.
- In some cases, simpler tools can play the role of ERP if designed carefully.

# Discussion

_**The role of culture, organisational structure and managers' mental models:**_
One of the most important findings of this research is the direct effect of organisational culture and managers' mental models on the success or failure of an ERP rollout. Traditional structures built on hierarchy and informal communication meet inner resistance when facing process-oriented ERP. A change in managers' attitude, cross-unit empowerment and acceptance of information transparency are key prerequisites for a successful transition.

_**Why ERP does not always work:**_
Many ERP failures come from early implementation without cultural and structural preparation. Organisations that have not yet documented their cross-unit processes, or whose information flow is manual and depends on key people, do not progress with ERP and even come close to crisis. ERP must answer a real structural need, not merely be a technological choice.

_**The place of the model in management decisions:**_
By giving stage-by-stage indicators and barriers to transition, the proposed model gives managers tools to measure the organisation's readiness. Managers can use the framework to find the organisation's current position and plan the path of software transformation purposefully. In effect the model is a bridge between the strategic view and operational execution in an organisation's digital transformation.

# Conclusion

## Summary of the model
The five-stage model of software transformation, from simple MIS to strategic ERP, is a tool for understanding precisely the maturity of information systems in organisations. It helps treat ERP as the end point of a path, not its starting point.

## Organisational and managerial uses
Using this model, organisations can:
- Identify the right time to enter ERP
- Phase the ERP project and implement it gradually
- Rebuild their organisational and cultural structure in line with digital transformation

# Practical recommendations

## A checklist for transformation managers

- Have the organisation's main processes been documented?
- Is data flow between units formal, transparent and traceable?
- Are teams ready to accept a matrix structure?
- Are decisions in the organisation made on data?

## Indicators of readiness for ERP

- Defined KPIs for key processes
- Current use of BI and data analysis tools
- Cultural maturity for cross-unit teamwork
- Few paper processes or phone-based communications in daily operations
