# People Power / Community Organizing

<small>Term: *People Power / Community Organizing* | Book: [[book-profile-deane-johns|Lipstick on a Pig (financial bailouts): book profile and conceptual summary]]</small>

## Definition and core idea

"People power" in the book's argument refers to the ability of citizens and civic groups to organise shared interests, influence public decisions and change institutions that do not normally answer sufficiently to users and society.

## Explanation and elaboration

The central idea is that the scattering of individuals can weaken their bargaining power, while organising, a shared narrative, distributed leadership and sustained action make it more likely that shared demands are raised and pursued. In the underlying text, Marshall Ganz's model and "public narrative" are presented as tools for mobilising collective action.

Marshall Ganz's research on public narrative describes it as a way of linking values, the story of "self", the story of "us" and the story of "now" to collective choice and action. This framework complements the analysis of organising, but it should not be presented without evidence as a model composed specifically in this book.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Separating claim from fact
> "People power" is a broad, normative term; real representation, accountability of leaders, financial transparency and participation of affected groups matter in order to prevent an organisation being captured by a narrow group.
