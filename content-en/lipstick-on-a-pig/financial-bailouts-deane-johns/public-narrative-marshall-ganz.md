# Public Narrative / Marshall Ganz

<small>Term: *Public Narrative / Marshall Ganz* | Book: [[book-profile-deane-johns|Lipstick on a Pig (financial bailouts): book profile and conceptual summary]]</small>

## Definition and core idea

Public narrative is a way of turning shared values and experiences into motivation and collective action. In Marshall Ganz's framework, a leader can link a story of "self", a story of "us" and a story of "now".

## Explanation and elaboration

The story of self explains why the issue matters to the individual; the story of us clarifies the shared values and experience of a group; and the story of now shows the situation or choice that makes action necessary. In Deane-Johns's book this kind of organising is presented as one of the tools for strengthening people power.

Applied to financial policy, a narrative has to be tied to economic evidence, clear interests and a practical path to change. Storytelling can engage people, but it does not replace analysis of costs, policy options and mechanisms of accountability.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Separating claim from fact
> Ganz's framework is a complementary theoretical source and should not be presented, without reference to the book's text, as a theory invented by Deane-Johns.
