# Direct Beneficiaries of Bailouts

<small>Term: *Direct Beneficiaries of Bailouts* | Book: [[book-profile-deane-johns|Lipstick on a Pig (financial bailouts): book profile and conceptual summary]]</small>

## Definition and core idea

Stakeholder analysis examines which groups receive the direct and indirect benefits of a bailout and which groups bear its costs.

## Explanation and elaboration

In the book's critical account, creditors of financial institutions and large debtors may benefit from financial-system support more than ordinary citizens, because supporting the continuity of a bank's obligations can preserve the value of claims. But for any given case this conclusion requires tracing the flow of funds, the capital structure and the conditions attached to the support.

A stakeholder map should include at least these groups: shareholders, managers, secured and unsecured creditors, depositors, borrowers, bank staff, other financial institutions, the state and taxpayers. "Rescuing a bank" may overlap with protecting depositors or keeping payments going, but these aims are not the same.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Separating claim from fact
> The claim that one group is the "main beneficiary" must be backed by evidence from that particular programme; the systemic effect of an intervention sometimes reaches households and businesses indirectly.
