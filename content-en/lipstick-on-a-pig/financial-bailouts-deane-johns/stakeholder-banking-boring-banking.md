# Stakeholder Banking / Boring Banking

<small>Term: *Stakeholder Banking / Boring Banking* | Book: [[book-profile-deane-johns|Lipstick on a Pig (financial bailouts): book profile and conceptual summary]]</small>

## Definition and core idea

Stakeholder banking, or "boring banking", refers here to models that focus on basic services, lending to the real economy and lasting relationships with depositors and borrowers, rather than maximising short-term profit through complex products and speculation.

## Explanation and elaboration

This view sees a bank as the infrastructure of service and credit allocation, not merely a platform for growing financial income. Cooperative, local or public banks may be examples of this logic, though ownership structure alone does not determine the quality of performance. Access to credit, cost of services, asset quality, stability of funding, risk management and accountability have to be weighed together.

"Boring" is a term of praise for a simple, understandable model of banking; it is not an invitation to avoid innovation or ignore technology.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Separating claim from fact
> Local or cooperative models can also suffer from weak management or concentration risk. Comparison should rest on performance, governance and regulation, not just the ownership label.
