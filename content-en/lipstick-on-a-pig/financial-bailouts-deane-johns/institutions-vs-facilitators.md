# Institutions vs. Facilitators

<small>Term: *Institutions vs. Facilitators* | Book: [[book-profile-deane-johns|Lipstick on a Pig (financial bailouts): book profile and conceptual summary]]</small>

## Definition and core idea

According to the book's introduction, one of its chapters distinguishes between "institutions", which spend much of their energy on solving their own problems, and "facilitators", which focus on solving their customers' problems.

## Explanation and elaboration

This distinction is a way to examine an organisation's orientation: are processes and products designed around the organisation's internal structure and its own survival interests, or around the user's real problem? A facilitator is not necessarily small or private; what matters is how the design of the service puts the user's need at the centre and what mechanism it has for learning and accountability.

In financial services this view can raise questions about product simplicity, transparent cost, access, fit with the customer's need, and the ability to switch between providers.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Separating claim from fact
> This summary relies on the publisher's description and the overview of the five chapters; the exact formulation and examples of the second chapter were not available in the underlying text, so this account is a cautious elaboration of the same distinction.
