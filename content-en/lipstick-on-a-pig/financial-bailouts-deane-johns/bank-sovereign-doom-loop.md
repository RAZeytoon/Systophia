# Bank–Sovereign Doom Loop

<small>Term: *Bank–Sovereign Doom Loop* | Book: [[book-profile-deane-johns|Lipstick on a Pig (financial bailouts): book profile and conceptual summary]]</small>

## Definition and core idea

The bank–sovereign loop, or the link between banks and the sovereign, refers to a negative feedback in which weakness in banks can weaken the government's finances, and a decline in the government's credit puts pressure in turn on banks' balance sheets, funding and stability.

## Explanation and elaboration

If the government takes on a large financial commitment to support banks, concern about the state of public debt can rise. On the other hand, banks that hold a large amount of government bonds may be hurt by a fall in the value of those bonds or a rise in the government's funding cost. Weakness in banks and in the government intensify each other in such a situation.

The IMF describes this link as a mechanism for transmitting and amplifying financial shocks. Even so, the loop does not work the same way in every country and every crisis; the level of debt, the structure of banks' assets, the currency of debt, monetary policy, market confidence and the government's fiscal capacity all matter.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Separating claim from fact
> The bank–sovereign loop is not solely the result of bailouts; balance-sheet links, holdings of government bonds, guarantees and the effect of a banking crisis on the economy and tax revenue also play a part.
