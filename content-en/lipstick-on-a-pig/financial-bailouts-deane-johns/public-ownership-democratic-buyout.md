# Public Ownership / Democratic Buyout

<small>Term: *Public Ownership / Democratic Buyout* | Book: [[book-profile-deane-johns|Lipstick on a Pig (financial bailouts): book profile and conceptual summary]]</small>

## Definition and core idea

In the book's argument, one of the proposed options, instead of unconditional aid to a private institution, is for the public sector to buy it or take it over, with a mission and long-term accountability.

## Explanation and elaboration

Public ownership may allow the state or a public body to factor aims such as local development finance, small businesses or environmental investment into decision-making. But ownership alone does not guarantee a public purpose; the type of governance, operational independence, oversight, transparency, limits on political interference and performance evaluation all matter.

Contemporary research on public banks shows that these institutions can play a role in financing and economic transitions, but they are neither inherently good nor inherently bad; the structure and the social forces that steer them shape the outcome.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Separating claim from fact
> The book's proposal for public ownership is the author's policy position. It should be weighed against other options such as bail-in, resolution, recapitalisation, orderly sale of assets and targeted support for depositors.
