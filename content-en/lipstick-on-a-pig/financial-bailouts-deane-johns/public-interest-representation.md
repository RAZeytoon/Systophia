# Public Interest Representation

<small>Term: *Public Interest Representation* | Book: [[book-profile-deane-johns|Lipstick on a Pig (financial bailouts): book profile and conceptual summary]]</small>

## Definition and core idea

Public-interest representation means that groups affected by the financial system, including depositors, borrowers, employees and local communities, can be reflected effectively and continuously in decision-making and oversight.

## Explanation and elaboration

The author pays attention to the role of civil-society organisations in linking scattered citizens with financial decision-makers. The idea addresses the problem of asymmetry in power and information: large lobbies usually have specialised resources and organised access, while the public interest may be scattered and uncoordinated.

Possible measures include public consultation, publication of data, independent representation, stakeholder councils, oversight of a public mission, and mechanisms for complaint and accountability. The design of any mechanism must expose conflicts of interest and make clear who actually has the power to decide.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Separating claim from fact
> Representation differs from symbolic presence or consultation without real influence; it should be clear how proposals are examined, what information is published and to whom decision-makers are accountable.
