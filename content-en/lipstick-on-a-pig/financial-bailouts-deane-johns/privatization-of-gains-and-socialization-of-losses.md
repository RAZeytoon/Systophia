# Privatization of Gains and Socialization of Losses

<small>Term: *Privatization of Gains and Socialization of Losses* | Book: [[book-profile-deane-johns|Lipstick on a Pig (financial bailouts): book profile and conceptual summary]]</small>

## Definition and core idea

This phrase points to a situation in which the profits of boom periods go mainly to private owners, managers or investors, while part of the losses of a crisis is passed on to society through public aid, state guarantees or economic recession.

## Explanation and elaboration

In the book's critique, the main problem is the asymmetry of responsibility and reward: if financial actors share in the profit but expect a large part of the loss to be transferred to the state in a crisis, the market's allocation of risk may break down. The public cost is not just the direct amount paid either; guarantees, the opportunity cost of resources and macroeconomic consequences can matter too.

For a careful analysis we have to distinguish shareholders, secured creditors, unsecured creditors, depositors, taxpayers and users of banking services. No intervention benefits or harms all these groups equally.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Separating claim from fact
> "Socialization of losses" is an important critical framework, but each case needs a documented distributional estimate; from the phrase alone we cannot conclude that all costs were passed to the public or that all gains stayed private.
