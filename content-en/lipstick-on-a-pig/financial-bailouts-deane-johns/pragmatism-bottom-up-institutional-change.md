# Pragmatism / Bottom-up Institutional Change

<small>Term: *Pragmatism / Bottom-up Institutional Change* | Book: [[book-profile-deane-johns|Lipstick on a Pig (financial bailouts): book profile and conceptual summary]]</small>

## Definition and core idea

In the book's introduction, pragmatism refers to a bottom-up tendency which, amid distrust of institutions, asks what actually works and how people can build solutions suited to their own needs.

## Explanation and elaboration

The book links part of the change to the personalisation of services and the emergence of participatory structures online. From this angle people are not merely consumers of institutional services; they can take part in producing, financing and governing solutions.

In institutional analysis, pragmatism does not mean setting theory aside; it means judging ideas by function, consequence and experience. A bottom-up approach can help local initiative and innovation, but generalisation, protecting rights, managing risk and coordinating infrastructure still need rules and oversight.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Separating claim from fact
> The use of the word "pragmatist" in this work is part of the author's conceptual frame; it should not be equated, without explanation, with all philosophical or political definitions of pragmatism.
