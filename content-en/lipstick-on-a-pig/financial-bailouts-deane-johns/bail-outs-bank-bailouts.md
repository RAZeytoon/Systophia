# Bail-outs / Bank Bailouts

<small>Term: *Bail-outs / Bank Bailouts* | Book: [[book-profile-deane-johns|Lipstick on a Pig (financial bailouts): book profile and conceptual summary]]</small>

## Definition and core idea

A bailout is an intervention by the state or a public body to prevent the collapse of a financial institution or to support the stability of the financial system. It may take the form of an investment, a loan, a debt guarantee or an asset purchase; these tools are not alike in cost, risk and distribution of benefits.

## Explanation and elaboration

Deane-Johns's book takes a critical view: in his belief, bailing out banks can preserve a troubled structure, shift the burden of risk onto society and reduce the incentive for deep reform. This argument must also be weighed against the possible aims of intervention: preventing contagion, protecting deposits and payment services, and avoiding a sharp contraction of credit.

So the useful question is not "are bailouts always good or bad?" but who receives help, who pays the losses, what conditions are imposed on the help, what responsibility shareholders and managers bear, and how the institution can be resolved without imposing disproportionate losses on ordinary people.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Separating claim from fact
> GAO's final report on the US TARP programme shows that its broad programme had a net cost overall; but that figure covers all programmes funded from TARP and does not by itself prove that every bank bailout lost money or succeeded. Evaluation has to be done programme by programme.
