# Lipstick on a Pig / Superficial Reform

<small>Term: *Lipstick on a Pig / Superficial Reform* | Book: [[book-profile-deane-johns|Lipstick on a Pig (financial bailouts): book profile and conceptual summary]]</small>

## Definition and core idea

In this book, "lipstick on a pig" is a critical metaphor for superficial reforms that change how the financial system looks while, in the author's claim, leaving untouched the basic mechanisms that produce risk, concentrate power and weaken accountability.

## Explanation and elaboration

Deane-Johns uses the metaphor against policies that, instead of redesigning financial incentives and structures, preserve the old shape of the system by injecting public money or repackaging regulation. The point of the metaphor is to ask: does the intervention really solve the problem, or does it merely buy time and shift the cost?

By itself the concept does not prove that every bailout or regulatory reform is useless. To assess a specific action you have to examine the aim of the intervention, the contract terms, how losses were distributed, the consequences for financial stability, and the reforms that followed.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Separating claim from fact
> The claim that a bailout is "merely cosmetic" is the author's critical thesis; it should not be generalised to all government interventions in banking crises without a case-by-case look.
