# The Timeless Way

<small>Original term: *The Timeless Way* | Chapter/location: 1; opening section | Chapter start page in the English edition's table of contents: 3</small>

## Definition and core idea

A way of building in which the order of an environment emerges from the interaction of human needs, the features of a place, materials and real forces, not merely from a formal picture that the designer imposes on it in advance.

## Explanation and elaboration

"Timeless" does not mean old or traditional in appearance. It means a generative, experience-based process that can produce habitable environments in many contexts. Alexander points to living traditions of building as examples of tacit knowledge and shared rules, not as a style to be copied unchanged.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Caution in attribution
> The existence of a single, timeless way is Alexander's theoretical thesis, not a truth everyone agrees on. Cultural, economic and climatic diversity and the needs of different groups must be taken into account in any assessment.
