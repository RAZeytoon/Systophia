# The Structure of a Language

<small>Original term: *The Structure of a Language* | Chapter/location: 16 | Chapter start page in the English edition's table of contents: 305</small>

## Definition and core idea

A pattern language is a network of related patterns. Completeness can refer both to covering spatial problems and relations and to responding to real needs and forces.

## Explanation and elaboration

Large-scale patterns, such as the structure of a neighbourhood, create the setting for medium and small-scale patterns. To judge a language, ask whether it covers the important problems, whether the relations are clear, whether differences of context can be absorbed, and whether users' experience can lead to revision.
