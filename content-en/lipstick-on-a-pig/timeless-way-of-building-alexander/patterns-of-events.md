# Patterns of Events

<small>Original term: *Patterns of Events* | Chapter/location: 4 | Chapter start page in the English edition's table of contents: 55</small>

## Definition and core idea

Patterns of events are the recurring behaviours and activities that take place in a setting: who does what, when, with whom and under what conditions. In Alexander's account a place gets its character from the patterns of events that keep happening there, not from its geometry alone.

## Explanation and elaboration

To analyse a place you have to ask who uses the space, when, and for what activity; which behaviours recur; which conditions make them easier, and who is left out of the space. Patterns of events are not separate from geometry, but they are not reducible to geometry either.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Caution in attribution
> Recurring events are not the same in all cultures. Age, physical ability, time, climate and social norms all affect a pattern.
