# Character of Nature vs. Modular Construction

<small>Original term: *Character of Nature vs. Modular Construction* | Chapter/location: relates to chapters 19 to 23 | Chapter start page in the English edition's table of contents: 365 to 455; exact location needs checking</small>

## Definition and core idea

Repeating a rule is different from mechanically copying identical parts. The underlying summary stresses that nature has both similarity and context-sensitive variety.

## Explanation and elaboration

In natural systems, parts can follow shared rules yet respond to different local conditions. In architecture too, modularity can raise productivity and repairability; the problem arises where rigid standardisation ignores the differences between situations and human experience.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Caution in attribution
> "Modularity kills space" is a sharp generalisation. This note confines it to a critique of context-blind applications of standardisation, not a blanket verdict against every modular system.
