# One Pattern at a Time

<small>Original term: *One Pattern at a Time* | Chapter/location: 20 | Chapter start page in the English edition's table of contents: 385</small>

## Definition and core idea

The principle of concentrating on one problem or pattern at each step of design, while keeping its relation to the whole and the order of dependence among patterns.

## Explanation and elaboration

Instead of solving all problems at once in a final picture, the designer examines one important pattern and then moves to the next. This can reduce cognitive complexity, provided the interaction of patterns and the possibility of revising earlier decisions are not forgotten.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Caution in attribution
> The right order does not guarantee that every later decision fits into the existing structure without conflict; implementation needs feedback and review.
