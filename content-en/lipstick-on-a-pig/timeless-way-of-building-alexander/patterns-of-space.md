# Patterns of Space

<small>Original term: *Patterns of Space* | Chapter/location: 5 | Chapter start page in the English edition's table of contents: 75</small>

## Definition and core idea

Recurring spatial relationships that can respond to human or environmental needs and forces. A pattern is not merely a visual form but a relationship between a context, a problem and a spatial arrangement.

## Explanation and elaboration

A pattern might concern the threshold between public and private space, the relation of a window to a seating place, or a building's connection to the street. To analyse one, ask what the context is, which forces conflict with each other, and what configuration could resolve them. This triad is a useful analytic formulation and should not be presented, unchecked, as a verbatim quotation from the book.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Caution in attribution
> A pattern needs a context. A solution that works in one climate, culture or scale may change elsewhere.
