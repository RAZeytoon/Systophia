# The No-Spin Era & Transparency

<small>Term: *The No-Spin Era & Transparency* | Book: [[book-profile-clarke|Lipstick on a Pig (the no-spin era): book profile and conceptual summary]]</small>

## Definition and main idea

Clarke argues that the growth in the number of information sources and the speed at which news circulates have made manipulating the narrative and concealment more fragile. Organisations and public figures cannot rely for long on one-way control of the news; stating the facts clearly is a more durable strategy.

## Explanation and elaboration

This claim has both an ethical and a practical side. When an organisation stays silent or delays bad news, others may build the first narrative. Transparency means giving correct, relevant and understandable information, together with an admission of the limits of what is known; it does not mean publishing all data aimlessly. Legitimate confidentiality, privacy and security still matter.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Source versus analysis
> The advice to be transparent should not be read as disclosing all information without exception. The main point is to avoid deceiving people about important facts and to explain clearly the limits of what can be published.
