# Credibility & Trust

<small>Term: *Credibility & Trust* | Book: [[book-profile-clarke|Lipstick on a Pig (the no-spin era): book profile and conceptual summary]]</small>

## Definition and main idea

Communication credibility is not just a good reputation or the ability to speak well; it is formed from consistency among words, evidence, behaviour and acceptance of responsibility.

## Explanation and elaboration

A well-made message can attract attention, but if it conflicts with the organisation's reality it is vulnerable in the long run. Admitting error, giving checkable facts and documenting corrective action can stop a mistake from turning into a deeper crisis of trust. The audience also pays attention to the organisation's record and how it behaves under pressure.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Source versus analysis
> Transparency does not guarantee that all audiences will trust or agree with the organisation's decision. The aim is not total control over others' reaction; it is providing honest information and making informed judgement possible.
