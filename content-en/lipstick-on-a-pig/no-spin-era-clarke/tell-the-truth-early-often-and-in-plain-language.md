# Tell the Truth Early, Often, and in Plain Language

<small>Term: *Tell the Truth Early, Often, and in Plain Language* | Book: [[book-profile-clarke|Lipstick on a Pig (the no-spin era): book profile and conceptual summary]]</small>

## Definition and main idea

The book's central principle is that truth should be told early, repeatedly and in clear language; communication should not be delayed until the preferred story is complete or until others reveal the news.

## Explanation and elaboration

"Early" means that verified facts are announced at the first responsible moment. "Often" means that information is updated as details become clear. "Plain" means the audience should not have to decode specialist language or defensive phrasing to understand the message. If something is still unknown, that uncertainty itself must be stated: what we know, what we do not know and what is being done to clarify the matter.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Source versus analysis
> Speed does not replace accuracy. This principle is not a licence to publish guesses or unverified information; fact, ambiguity and conjecture must stay separate.
