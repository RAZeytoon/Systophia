# Don't Create a Pig in the First Place

<small>Term: *Don't Create a Pig in the First Place* | Book: [[book-profile-clarke|Lipstick on a Pig (the no-spin era): book profile and conceptual summary]]</small>

## Definition and main idea

The best way to avoid dressing up reality is not to create the flawed situation in the first place. Good communication cannot permanently hide the real weakness of a product, decision or policy, or unethical behaviour.

## Explanation and elaboration

If a product is flawed or a decision has harmful effects, producing a positive message does not replace fixing the problem. Before drafting the message one should ask: is the decision defensible? Are the promises consistent with actual performance? Should we fix the problem, or do we only want to change how it looks? Strategic communication must therefore be tied to the quality of operations, ethics, decision-making and risk management.

## Notes and limits of interpretation

> [!note] Source versus analysis
> This principle does not mean communication is unimportant. A good decision may also be misunderstood and need explanation; but the explanation should clarify performance, not replace it.
