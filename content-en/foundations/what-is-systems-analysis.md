> [!question] What are we looking at?
> What exactly is "systems analysis", and why does it get more necessary every year?

# Why systems analysis?

The problems in front of us today are rarely simple or one-dimensional. Making the right decision, designing a policy that actually works, improving an organisation's process, or even understanding a social phenomenon all call for a view that sees the whole and doesn't settle for one corner of the story. Systems analysis exists for exactly this point.

I think systems analysis is one of the most basic subjects in engineering, management and social systems, because it teaches us to look at a subject not from one component or one specialty, but from above and in the interaction of its parts. The aim of this introductory course is the same: to get to know the basic concepts of system, analysis, the relation of components and the role purpose plays in shaping structures, so that we can build a sound analytical approach to complex problems.

# Two first definitions

**What is a system?** A set of components that are connected and work for a specific purpose. Components can be physical or non-physical, human or non-human, visible or invisible. The key point of the definition is that **there are connections and a purpose between the components.**

**What is analysis?** "Decompose, then connect." Contrary to the common idea, analysis is not just breaking a problem into smaller pieces; its art is that after decomposing, we rebuild the relations between the pieces and arrive at a new understanding of the whole. In systems analysis we don't only want to know the components, we want to understand how they work together to reach one coherent result.

# Where does it apply?

Systems analysis is not a purely theoretical or academic concept. Wherever there is a problem with several parts that affect each other, it belongs:

- designing an organisation's structure
- analysing economic or environmental crises
- optimising production or service processes
- designing information and technology systems
- understanding complex social phenomena such as poverty, migration or public policy

In all of these, a systems view helps us decide better and see unintended consequences earlier.

# Why does it matter more for the future?

The future is moving fast towards more complexity, and we can no longer solve problems with a linear, traditional view. Artificial intelligence, climate change, emerging technologies, social networks, data governance and the like are phenomena that can be neither understood nor managed without a systematic view. Systems analysis prepares us to think in several dimensions, find hidden connections and foresee possible futures, and I suspect these abilities will be an advantage for every person, every organisation and even every country in tomorrow's world.
